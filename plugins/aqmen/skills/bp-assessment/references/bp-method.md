# Business plan assessment — the method

The question a buyer asks about a management business plan is not "is it
right" but **"how optimistic is it, where, and why"**. The assessment answers
that assumption by assumption, on one scale, with the evidence, so the deal
team can adjust the plan rather than argue about it.

## 1. Decompose the plan into assumptions, not lines

A plan is usually presented as revenue and EBITDA by year, split by
initiative, geography or business line. Two things to build first:

- **Growth channels** — the components of growth as management presents them
  (e.g. constant perimeter, adviser recruitment, lead tech, M&A), grouped
  where they behave alike (organic vs inorganic), with each channel's share of
  the end-year revenue and EBITDA. The **bridge** from year 1 to year N by
  channel is the context slide: it shows where the plan's growth actually
  comes from and how much sits with channels that have no track record.
- **Assumptions** — the drivers that run across channels (volumes, prices,
  penetration, productivity, costs), grouped into three or four categories
  ("how assets get sourced", "the service they hire", "how much they pay").
  These are the rows of the assessment. Typical count: 6–10.

Then run a **sensitivity**: change each assumption by ±10% and record the
effect on end-year EBITDA (absolute and as a share). The assumptions with the
biggest swing get the deep dives.

## 2. Rate every assumption on three lenses

For each assumption ask, with evidence:

| Lens | Question | Typical evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Market** | Is the assumed growth / level in line with what the market has done and is expected to do? | Market model, trends, peers' disclosed growth, analyst data |
| **Competitive position** | Given where the company sits (share, pricing, proposition), is the assumption achievable? | Benchmarks vs peers, KPCs, pricing surveys |
| **Track record** | Has the company delivered this before, at this pace? | Historicals, cohort data, past initiatives, management's own hit rate |

Rate each lens and then the assumption overall on the fixed scale:

| Rating | Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Highly conservative | ++ | Materially below what the evidence supports on all lenses |
| Conservative | + | Below what market, position and track record justify |
| Realistic | = | In line with market, position and history |
| Optimistic | − | Above what at least one lens supports; needs execution not yet shown |
| Highly optimistic | −− | Beyond what market, position or track record supports; a step-change |
| Not applicable | N/A | Not assessed in detail; carried at plan value (say so) |

The overall rating is not an average: one lens can dominate (a recruitment
plan that needs 2.9× the historical hiring rate is highly optimistic whatever
the market says). Write the rationale in two or three sentences that name the
number in the plan, the number in the evidence, and the gap.

## 3. Deep-dive the assumptions that matter

For the 3–6 assumptions with the largest sensitivity or the most contested
rating, one slide each: the plan's number, the evidence (historicals, peers,
market) in one or two exhibits, and the assessment with its rating. Headline
in the form "N. <Assumption> deemed <rating> because <the gap>".

## 4. State Aqmen's own view

For every assumption rated other than realistic, give the value Aqmen would
use instead and why. That is what lets the deal team build an adjusted case;
without it the assessment is commentary.

## 5. Conclude on the plan as a whole

One headline: "Assessment of key assumptions implies the business plan is
<slightly optimistic / realistic / …>", with the two or three assumptions that
drive that conclusion and what they are worth in end-year EBITDA.

## Rules

- Every rating has a rationale with numbers; a rating without evidence is an
  opinion.
- Separate what was **assessed in detail** from what was **carried at plan
  value**; mark the latter N/A and say so.
- Reconcile: the sum of the channels equals the plan; the adjusted case
  reconciles to the assessment.
- Keep management's labels for channels and assumptions so the client can map
  the assessment back to their model.
